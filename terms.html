What INR Notebook is, and is not

INR Notebook is a personal record book for people taking warfarin. It keeps your INR results, the dose instructions your clinic gives you, the doses you take, your clinic visits and your reminders together.

It is not a medical device and it does not give medical advice. The app never calculates, suggests or changes a warfarin dose, and it never tells you what to do about a result. Where it shows a result against your target range, it is comparing numbers you entered; that is not a clinical judgement.

  • Always follow the instructions of your doctor, pharmacist or anticoagulation clinic. If you are unsure what to do, contact them.
  • In an emergency, including any of the danger signs of bleeding, contact your local emergency services or go to a hospital straight away. Do not wait to check the app.
  • Do not rely on reminders alone. Phones restart, batteries run out and notifications can be turned off.

Information from hospital documents

The danger signs, the warfarin card notice and the vitamin K food list in the app are transcribed from hospital documents, and each screen says where its information came from. They are there for reference and may not match what your own clinic tells you. Your clinic's instructions come first.

Meal photos analysed by AI

If you set up an AI service with your own key, the app can send a meal photo to that service to name the foods and estimate their nutrients. Those names, amounts and nutrients are estimates made by that service. They can be wrong, they are not from your clinic, and they are not medical or dietary advice. Where a food is on your clinic's food list, the app shows the vitamin K level from the list instead of the AI's estimate. The same service can read the name and strength off a photo of a medicine's packet, to fill in your health diary; what it reads can be wrong, so check it against the packet before saving. The app says nothing about how a medicine mixes with warfarin. Your use of the AI service is between you and that service, under its terms, including any charges to your account.

Your records

  • The app shows what you enter, so check your entries against your clinic's records.
  • Your records are kept on your phone. If the phone is lost, reset or replaced, or the app is uninstalled, without a backup somewhere else, your records can be lost for good. Uninstalling deletes everything the app stored, including the backups it keeps on the phone.
  • Keep your recovery code safe. Without it a backup cannot be restored on another phone, and we cannot recover it for you.
