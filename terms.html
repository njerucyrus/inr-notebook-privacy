What INR Notebook is, and is not

INR Notebook is a personal record book for people taking warfarin. It keeps your INR results, the dose instructions your clinic gives you, the doses you take, your clinic visits and your reminders together.

It is not a medical device and it does not give medical advice. The app never calculates, suggests or changes a warfarin dose, and it never tells you what to do about a result. Where it shows a result against your target range, it is comparing numbers you entered; that is not a clinical judgement.

  • Always follow the instructions of your doctor, pharmacist or anticoagulation clinic. If you are unsure what to do, contact them.
  • In an emergency, including any of the danger signs of bleeding, contact your local emergency services or go to a hospital straight away. Do not wait to check the app.
  • Do not rely on reminders alone. Phones restart, batteries run out and notifications can be turned off.

Information from hospital documents

The danger signs, the warfarin card notice and the vitamin K food list in the app are transcribed from hospital documents, and each screen says where its information came from. The vitamin K food list is a Kenyan hospital's, and shows on phones in Kenya, or where you turn it on in Settings. They are there for reference and may not match what your own clinic tells you. Your clinic's instructions come first.

Smart Scan: meal and medicine photos read by AI

Smart Scan sends a meal photo, or the foods you type in, to OpenAI or to Google's Gemini, which are AI services, through INR Notebook's own server, to name the foods and estimate their nutrients. Those names, amounts and nutrients are estimates made by AI. They can be wrong, they are not from your clinic, and they are not medical or dietary advice. Where the Kenyan food list shows and a food is on it, the app shows the vitamin K level from the list instead of the AI's estimate. Smart Scan can also read the name and strength off a photo of a medicine's packet, to fill in your health diary; what it reads can be wrong, so check it against the packet before saving. The app says nothing about how a medicine mixes with warfarin.

  • Smart Scan needs a Google sign-in, used only to count your scans.
  • You get 10 scans a month, starting again on the 1st. A scan is one meal photo, one meal typed in, or one medicine packet; putting a food's name or amount right afterwards is free. A scan that gets no answer is given back.
  • We may change how many scans there are a month, or pause Smart Scan, for example when a service is unavailable or is being misused. Smart Scan may not always be available.
  • You can delete your Smart Scan account at any time, as the privacy policy describes.

Your records

  • The app shows what you enter, so check your entries against your clinic's records.
  • Your records are kept on your phone. If the phone is lost, reset or replaced, or the app is uninstalled, without a backup somewhere else, your records can be lost for good. Uninstalling deletes everything the app stored, including the backups it keeps on the phone.
  • Keep your recovery code safe. Without it a backup cannot be restored on another phone, and we cannot recover it for you.

How the app handles your information is described in the privacy policy.
