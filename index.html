Privacy policy — INR Notebook

Last updated: 19 November 2026

INR Notebook is an Android app for people taking warfarin. It keeps your INR results, your clinic's dose instructions, the doses you take and your clinic visits in one place. This policy explains what the app does with that information.

The short version

Your health records stay on your phone. There is no analytics, advertising or tracking of any kind, and we never receive your records. Nothing in the app needs an account, except Smart Scan, which uses a Google sign-in only to count your scans.

Only these things can leave your phone, and only in the ways described below: an encrypted backup copy, if you choose a folder to send it to; what Google Play needs to handle a subscription or a purchase; a photo of a meal or of a medicine's packet, and food names, if you use Smart Scan; for Smart Scan's extra scans and Premium's scans, a purchase's token, for our server to check with Google Play; and, the first time the app is opened, a request for the length of the free trial.

What the app stores, and where

INR Notebook keeps the following in its own private storage on your device:

  • Health records: INR results and target ranges, dose instructions from your clinic, the doses you record as taken or missed, clinic visits and appointments, the questions you note for your next visit, symptoms and notes you type, and the change history of each INR result and clinic visit
  • Your details: anything you choose to enter on the profile and clinic screens, such as your name, date of birth, hospital, file number, reason for taking warfarin, your clinic's contact details and any other contacts you add, such as your pharmacy, and a profile photo if you add one
  • Health diary: symptoms you note, with any photo you take for one; events such as an illness or travel; the medicines you take besides warfarin; the weights and height you enter; and your blood pressure, pulse, oxygen level (SpO2) and temperature readings, with any limits for blood pressure and pulse you enter from your clinic
  • Meals: your week of meals, any meal plans made before, your food log (the photos you take, what you type in, when you ate and how much), and what Smart Scan said about them
  • Smart Scan: the day you agreed to Smart Scan sending photos, and the last count of your scans the server gave
  • Reminders: the reminders you turn on and their times
  • Documents: photos and PDFs you attach, such as your warfarin card, copied into the app's storage
  • Settings: theme and notification choices, whether to show the Kenyan food list, the usual meals you chose and, if you set an app lock, a salted hash of your PIN (never the PIN itself)
  • Subscription status: the day your free trial started and how long it is, and whether Google Play last reported an active subscription

This information is not uploaded to us, not shared with anyone and not sold. We never see it.

Backups

  • Backup on your phone. When you tap Back up now, and once a day during the free trial or with INR Notebook Premium, the app saves everything above into a single encrypted file (AES-256-GCM, with the key held in the Android Keystore). It is kept in the app's storage on the phone.
  • Weekly copy to a folder you choose. If you choose a folder, for example in Google Drive, the newest backup is copied there each week, or when you tap Send now. The file is encrypted before it leaves your phone, so the service holding it cannot read your records. That service keeps the file under its own terms, and it stays there until you delete it.
  • Recovery code. A 64-digit recovery code lets you restore a backup on a new phone. Keep it somewhere safe: we do not have it and cannot recover it for you. The app keeps a copy locked by the Android Keystore, so the weekly copy can run without asking you for it. The Backup screen can share the code through Android's share sheet, for example to your own email, only when you tap Share; anyone who has both the code and a backup file can read your records, so send it only to yourself.
  • Android's own backup is switched off for INR Notebook, so Google's device backup does not copy your records.

Smart Scan: meal and medicine photos

Smart Scan names the foods on a meal photo and estimates what is in them, and reads a medicine's name and strength off a photo of its packet to fill in your health diary. It is off until you sign in to it with Google and agree to what it sends. You get 10 scans a month, or 30 with Premium, and can buy packs of extra scans.

  • Your Google account. Signing in uses Firebase Authentication, run by Google. INR Notebook receives your name, email address and a Google account id, and uses them only to count your scans. The rest of the app never needs an account.
  • What our server keeps. INR Notebook's own server (Google Firebase, in Belgium) keeps, for each account: how many scans were used this month, how many were given back, any extra scans, and for each scan which feature it was for, which service answered and when. These scan records are deleted after 35 days. It keeps no health records, no photos and no food names.
  • Extra scans and Premium. When you buy a pack of extra scans, and when you have Premium while signed in, the app sends our server the purchase's token from Google Play. The server asks Google Play about it: which pack or subscription, whether it was paid for or refunded, and until when a subscription runs. It keeps, for each pack: the pack, Google Play's order number, the scans it added and any taken back after a refund, and whether it was a test purchase; and, for Premium, until when it runs. A token is kept only as a one-way code, never as it is. Each pack is tagged at Google Play with a one-way code made from your Smart Scan account, not your name or email, so that it can only be added to that account.
  • What is sent: the meal photo, reduced in size, and, where the Kenyan food list shows, the names of foods on it so the service can use the same names; elsewhere, the names of foods you added yourself. To look up nutrients, only food names are sent. For a medicine, only the photo of its packet, reduced in size; the photo is not kept on your phone afterwards. Nothing else from your records is sent: no name, no INR results, no doses.
  • Who reads it: OpenAI, through INR Notebook's server, or Google's Gemini, asked from your phone through Google Firebase. Both run in the United States. Our server passes a request on and keeps nothing of it. OpenAI handles what it receives under its privacy policy, and Google under the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Your agreement first: before anything is first sent, the app names OpenAI and Google, says what is sent, and asks you to agree. Nothing is sent if you do not. You can withdraw in Settings, under Smart Scan, and it asks again before anything more is sent.
  • When: only when you take or choose a photo, type in or add a food, or rename one. A photo read before is not sent again, and a food read before is not asked about again; the saved answer is used.
  • Checking it is the real app. So that only INR Notebook can use our server, the app asks Google Play Integrity, through Firebase App Check, to confirm it is the genuine app on a genuine phone. This sends Google technical information about the app and the phone, never your records.
  • What comes back (food names, amounts and nutrient estimates) is stored with your other records, and included in your backups and exports. A medicine's name and strength are filled into the form for you to check, and kept only if you save it.
  • Deleting your Smart Scan account. In the app, go to Settings, then Smart Scan, then Delete this account; or write to us from the account's email address (see Delete your Smart Scan account). We delete the counts, any extra scans left, Premium's end date and the sign-in, within 30 days if you write to us. The record of each pack bought is kept, without your account, so that the same purchase can't be added twice. Your records on the phone are not touched. Signing out leaves your account on our server until you delete it.

What the app sends over the internet

  • Google Play, for Premium and extra scans. INR Notebook Premium and Smart Scan's packs of extra scans are sold through Google Play Billing. Google Play takes the payment; the app only learns what you have bought and whether a payment is pending, and confirms each purchase back to Google Play. For Smart Scan, our server checks a purchase with Google Play, as described above. We never receive your card, mobile money or other payment details. Google handles purchase information under the Google Play Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy.
  • The backup copy, if you choose a folder, through the storage app you picked.
  • Nothing, for ratings and sharing. Now and then the app may show Google Play's own rating card, and Rate in the menu opens the app's page in the Play Store. A rating or review you write there goes to Google Play from your Google account, as one written in the Play Store would; the app never sees it, and Google Play does not tell the app whether you rated. Share in the menu hands your phone's share sheet the app's name and its Play Store link, and nothing else.
  • Smart Scan: your Google sign-in, the scan count, purchase tokens for extra scans and Premium, and meal photos, food names and medicine packet photos, to Google Firebase, OpenAI and Google, as described above. Firebase's own libraries may also send Google limited technical information about how they are working, never your records.
  • The free trial's length. The first time the app is opened, it asks Firebase Remote Config, run by Google, how long the free trial is. This sends a random installation id made for the app, and nothing about you.

Nothing else. The app contains no analytics, crash reporting or advertising code, and the published app writes no logs.

Sharing your records

Only when you choose to. The Reports screen, part of INR Notebook Premium after the free trial, makes a PDF report, such as the one for your next clinic visit, or a CSV spreadsheet on your phone. It shows you every page of a report before anything leaves the phone, and then opens Android's share sheet, the print screen or the screen for saving a file, so you decide who receives it and where it goes. Exported files contain your health information, so share them only with people you trust, such as your clinic.

Sending a result to your clinic. Send to your clinic, on an INR result, writes a message with that result and its target range, your clinic's warfarin instruction before the test, your name, age, file number and height if you entered them, and any vitals you recorded in the week up to the test (blood pressure, pulse, oxygen level, temperature and weight), and shows it to you in full. If you choose your clinic or another contact you saved, the app opens WhatsApp with the message written into the chat with their number. You can also choose a chat in WhatsApp, send it with another app through Android's share sheet, or copy it. Nothing is sent until you send it there, and WhatsApp, or the app you chose, then carries it under its own terms.

Adding to your calendar. When you tap Add to your calendar beside an INR test or clinic appointment, the app opens your phone's calendar app with the date and "INR test" or "Clinic appointment" filled in. The app does not read or change your calendar and needs no calendar permission. The event is saved only if you save it there, and then your calendar app keeps it, and may sync it to your calendar account, under its own terms.

Keeping your records safe

Your records are protected by your phone's own lock screen and storage encryption, by the encryption of every backup and, if you turn it on, by the app lock. If you also choose to show your emergency card at the app lock, anyone who opens the app on your phone can read that card without your PIN: that you take warfarin, why, your target range, your other medicines and the numbers on it. Nothing else in the app can be opened from there. If you choose to show your emergency card on your phone's lock screen, a notice there says that you take warfarin, and tapping it opens the card without unlocking the phone, so anyone holding your phone can read it; nothing else on the phone is unlocked by it. The emergency card can also be copied, by you, into your phone's own medical information, which your phone shows on its lock screen; the app never puts it there itself. Keep your phone locked, and keep your recovery code private.

Children

INR Notebook is meant for adults managing their own warfarin treatment, or a parent or carer doing it for someone. It is not directed at children under 13. Smart Scan's sign-in is the only personal information our server receives, from anyone, of any age.

Deleting your data, and your rights

Because your records are on your phone and not with us, you are in control of them:

  • Delete any record inside the app at any time. Correcting some records, and exporting a copy, need INR Notebook Premium after the free trial; nothing you have recorded is ever hidden from you.
  • Uninstalling INR Notebook deletes everything it stored on the phone, including the daily backups kept there.
  • Backup copies in a folder you chose stay there until you delete them.
  • Subscription records are held by Google. Manage or cancel a subscription in the Play Store, under Payments and subscriptions.
  • Your Smart Scan account, its counts and its sign-in can be deleted in the app or on request; see Delete your Smart Scan account.

We hold no copy of your records, so there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete on your behalf beyond your Smart Scan account. You can still write to us with any question about your information, including under the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019.
